# West African Coastal & Pre-European Maritime Routes

Companion document to `SKILL.md`. Documents routes that **are not
covered by SlaveVoyages / AfricanOrigins / atlantic-voyages curated
routes** because they are either pre-European (before 1450), short
intra-African cabotage, or parallel circuits to the trans-Atlantic
voyages.

Use this document to confront the **hypotheses generated by MTBC L5/L6
phylogeography** (notably the Akan-Gabon route inferred in phase 26
of L5L6-codivergence) against documented coastal mobility.

## 1. Pre-European maritime routes (≤1450 CE)

### 1.1 Senegambia-Mauritania cabotage

**Period** : ~1000 BP onward (continuous to present).
**Actors** : Wolof, Serer, Lebu, Imraguen fishermen.
**Vessels** : pirogues (single-hulled wooden canoes), small lateen-sail
boats from ~1300 CE following Islamic contact.
**Route** : Cape Verde (mainland) – Saint-Louis – Banc d'Arguin –
Nouadhibou.
**Function** : fishing, salt trade, local exchange.

**Documentary sources** : Al-Bakri (1067), Al-Idrisi (1154), pottery
sequences of Senegal River valley archaeology (McIntosh & McIntosh
1980, 1993).

### 1.2 Sierra Leone-Liberia-Côte d'Ivoire (Pepper Coast / Grain Coast)

**Period** : ~1200-1450 CE.
**Actors** : Mel, Mande-Tan (Bullom, Temne, Mende), Vai, Kru.
**Route** : Bance estuary – Sherbro – Cape Mount – Cape Palmas –
Sassandra River.
**Vessels** : pirogues côtières + dugout canoes Kru (60+ paddles).
**Function** : iron and salt trade, malagueta pepper (Aframomum
melegueta) exchange.

**Documentary sources** : Portuguese factor reports XVe siècle
documentent un cabotage déjà bien établi à leur arrivée (Diogo Gomes,
Cadamosto, Pacheco Pereira).

### 1.3 Gulf of Guinea — Akan to Yoruba (≤1450)

**Period** : ~1100-1450 CE.
**Actors** : Akan, Ga-Dangme, Ewe/Gbe, Yoruba (Awori, Egba), Edo, Ijaw.
**Route** : Elmina-Anomabu – Volta-Keta-Aného – Ouidah-Allada – Lagos
lagoon – Niger Delta (Brass, Bonny, Calabar).
**Vessels** : pirogues lagunaires + canoes côtières.
**Function** : kola nut (Cola acuminata, C. nitida) exchange entre
zones forestières productrices et zones plus sèches ; or de l'arrière-pays
Akan (Bono-Ahafo) vers les ports yoruba ; salt and dried fish.

**Documentary sources** : Pacheco Pereira (1505), Akan oral tradition
(Asante-Akwapim Stool histories), archéologie de Begho, Bono-Manso,
Ife.

### 1.4 Bight of Biafra — Igbo-Efik-Cameroon (≤1450)

**Period** : ~1000-1450 CE.
**Actors** : Igbo (Aro), Efik, Ibibio, Duala, Bafia.
**Route** : Bonny-New Calabar-Cross River-Rio del Rey-Wouri estuary.
**Function** : palm oil, slaves (Aro Oracle trade) bien avant la traite
atlantique, cuivre.

**Documentary sources** : Northrup 1978, Latham 1973, Lovejoy 1986,
Ekejiuba 2008.

### 1.5 Loango-Kongo-Angola cabotage (≤1450)

**Period** : ~800-1450 CE (pre-Portuguese).
**Actors** : Vili, Bakongo, Mbundu, Ovimbundu.
**Route** : Cabinda-Loango-Soyo-Luanda-Benguela.
**Vessels** : pirogues marines (mokunda), grandes pirogues à voile.
**Function** : sel solaire (côtes d'Angola), nzimbu shells (monnaie
Kongo), cuivre (Cataracts), bois exotique.

**Documentary sources** : Vansina 1990, Heywood 2002, MacGaffey 1986,
archéologie Mbanza Kongo (Mbafa-Mbatí, Cuca, etc.).

### 1.6 The hypothesised "Akan-Gabon" connection (phase 26 hypothesis)

**Phase 26 of L5L6-codivergence project** identifies a coherent sub-clade
L5.2.2.1.1.2.1.2 (8 Gabon-specific strains, 76 cohesive SPDIs) within
L5.2.2.1.1.2.1 (otherwise Tano/Akan dominant). The two share the same
5 core L5.2.2.1.1.2.1 markers, suggesting a common ancestor.

**Direct Akan-Gabon maritime route** (Elmina → Libreville, ~1500 km
open sea) is **historically improbable** before 1500 CE :
- Open-sea navigation across the Gulf of Guinea was not common before
  European ships
- No documented voyages by Akan, Bantu_NW or Yoruba pre-1450 over
  this distance
- Currents (Guinea Current eastward + Benguela Current northward)
  would favour Cape Verde → Liberia → Gold Coast → Bight of Benin
  in successive coastal hops, then long detour via Gabon-Cameroon

**Alternative hypotheses** for the L5.2.2.1.1.2.1.2 Gabon cohesive
sub-clade :

**H1 — Multi-hop coastal cabotage** (5+ steps, several centuries) :
Akan → Bight of Benin → Niger Delta → Cameroon coast → Gabon estuary.
Each step is documented (cf. 1.3, 1.4, 1.5 above). The chain would
take several generations to deliver TB transmission westward to
eastward.

**H2 — Trans-equatorial pre-Bantu connection** (the "proto-Volta-Congo"
hypothesis) : a now-extinct or absorbed population once spanned both
the Akan-Tano area and the Bantu_NW/Gabon area before the Bantu
expansion (3 500-2 500 BP) compartmentalized them. MTBC L5.2.2.1.1.2.1
would predate the Bantu expansion and retain its ancestral coastal
distribution.

**H3 — Indirect via Iberian / Portuguese ship circulation (1450-1700)**
: Akan and Gabonese populations were both subject to Portuguese
visitation. Slaves and labourers could have been transported on
the São Tomé–Loango–Gabon cabotage circuit (well-documented for
1500-1700 CE by Heywood 2002, Vansina 1990) — São Tomé as
reception hub, Loango / Cap Lopez / Corisco as continental stops.

**Preferred hypothesis (revised 2026-05-11 after molecular clock
dating in phase 32bis)** : **H3** — Portuguese-mediated cabotage
transplant.

**Evidence (L5L6-codivergence phase 32bis, n=201 strains)** :

- Pairwise SNP distance between the 8 Gabon strains and their
  sister-clade (Akan-divergent L5.2.2.1.1.2.1.1.2, n=147) : median
  387 SNPs (IQR 339–419)
- TMRCA Gabon ↔ sister at clock = 0.44 SNP/genome/yr (Menardo 2019
  MTBC calibration) : ~440 BP (≈1586 CE)
- Range over plausible clock rates 0.30–0.55 : 352–645 BP
  (1381–1674 CE), the Portuguese exploration and early-cabotage window
- TMRCA intra-Gabon clade : ~245 BP (≈1781 CE), range 196–360 BP
  (1666–1830 CE) — squarely within the active Atlantic slave-trade
  window
- Tip-date estimates available for 196/201 strains (30 high-confidence,
  166 approximate via Coscolla 2021 Table S1 and source-paper sampling
  ranges, phase 33)

**Interpretation** : the 76 cohesive SPDIs of L5.2.2.1.1.2.1.2 are
**not a pre-Bantu archive** but the product of ~250 years of
intra-Gabon drift in a geographically isolated population (Ogooué
delta and Mpongwe hinterland), following introduction from an
Akan-divergent source via Portuguese cabotage.

**H2 (pre-Bantu) is robustly refuted** : it would require a
molecular clock rate < 0.10 SNP/genome/year, a value not documented
anywhere in the MTBC literature (published values range 0.15–0.55,
centred on 0.40–0.45).

**Scientific significance** : this is a clean case study of
African-to-African slave-trade transport documented genetically
in M. africanum, strengthening the "Atlantic-transplantation"
narrative for L5/L6 phylogeography.

### 1.7 São Tomé / Príncipe and Cape Verde as connectors

**Period** : 1470 onward (post-Portuguese settlement).
**Function** : São Tomé and Príncipe (settled 1470, sugar economy)
served as **slave reception hubs** before trans-Atlantic shipment.
Cape Verde (settled 1456-1462) served as **slave processing** for
Senegambia trade. These islands are coastal cabotage nodes despite
being off-shore.

Importantly, in our phase 18bis verification, **Cabo Verde has ZERO
L5/L6 strains sequenced in NCBI BioSample** (138 837 entries). Despite
its central role in the early Atlantic trade, the islands have NOT
preserved the M. africanum signature. This is consistent with :
- Local extinction of L5/L6 due to islander population bottlenecks
- L4 European replacement post-1500 (consistent with the broader
  "extinction + replacement" hypothesis of P5)
- Sub-sampling artefact (no recent TB sequencing project on the islands)

## 2. Parallel routes during the Atlantic slave trade (1450-1870)

### 2.1 The "Costa da Mina" return circuit (Bahia ↔ Bight of Benin)

**Period** : 1690-1850.
**Function** : Brazilian merchants (especially from Bahia) developed
a **direct circuit** with Ouidah and Lagos that bypassed the
"triangular trade" Europe-Africa-Americas. Slaves embarked at Ouidah
were paid for partly in **Brazilian tobacco** (third-quality "soca")
and rum. Some freed slaves and their descendants (**Aguda /
Brazilians**) returned to Lagos/Ouidah after 1835, founding the
"Brazilian quarter" of Lagos.

This is a **bi-directional route**, not just one-way deportation.
Has implications for L5/L6 diaspora : Aguda returnees could have
carried Brazilian-acquired MTBC (L4) back to Africa, and conversely
some L5/L6 lineages reached Brazil directly from Bight of Benin
without intermediate ports.

### 2.2 São Tomé-Angola circuit (Portuguese 1500-1700)

**Period** : 1500-1700.
**Function** : São Tomé as the **main slave reception and trans-shipment
hub** for the Portuguese Atlantic. Slaves embarked at Loango, Cabinda,
Luanda → São Tomé → Brazil (Bahia, Pernambuco). The island served
multiple coastal sources : São Tomé itself, Príncipe, and the
nearby continental coast (Gabon, Loango).

### 2.3 Cape Verde-Senegambia circuit (1450-1830)

**Period** : 1450-1830.
**Function** : Cape Verde (Ribeira Grande, then Praia after 1770) as
**slave reception hub** for Senegambian sources (Wolof, Serer, Mande
captives). Then trans-shipment to Caribbean (Hispaniola, Cuba),
Brazil (Pernambuco, Bahia), and metropolitan Portugal.

### 2.4 Inter-American coastwise (1626-1860)

Covered by `atlantic-voyages intra-american` command. Includes :
- Re-shipment between Caribbean islands (Jamaica → Bahamas →
  Cuba → Mexico)
- Coastwise USA (Charleston → New Orleans, Norfolk → Charleston)
- Brazil internal (Bahia → Rio after gold cycle, Bahia → Pará for
  rubber boom 1850-1910)

Volumes : ~11 000 voyages documented.

## 3. Trans-Saharan terrestrial routes (≤1820 CE)

These are not maritime but **complete the West African mobility
picture** for MTBC phylogeography.

| Route | Period | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| **Western** : Sijilmassa-Awdaghust-Tichit-Wagadou | ~700-1500 | Magreb → Ghana antique |
| **Central** : Tripoli-Ghadames-Kawar-Kanem-Bornou | ~1000-1850 | Maghreb → Lake Chad |
| **Eastern** : Egypt-Fezzan-Bornou-Hausaland | ~700-1800 | Mediterranean → Hausa |
| **Mali-Niger** : Tombouctou-Djenné-Walata-Niani | ~1100-1500 | Mali interior |
| **Songhay** : Tombouctou-Gao-Tahoua-Air | ~1450-1591 | Songhay |
| **Trans-Volta** : Mossi-Hausa-Yoruba (kola+gold) | ~1200-1850 | Niger-Congo internal |

**Implication for MTBC** : Saharan caravan routes brought slaves
**northward** to Mediterranean (~1.5-2 million over 13 centuries,
Austen 1979). If captives came from Niger-Congo regions, MTBC
L5/L6 could theoretically be transported to North Africa, Mediterranean,
Middle East. Worth checking : the 116 France L5/L6 + 79 Italie L5/L6
in diaspora could include some descendants of trans-Saharan slaves
via Maghreb (rather than only modern migration). At 50% Maghrebi
descent in the Sahel-to-Tripoli corridor, this is plausible but
to be tested.

## 4. Hypotheses generated by L5/L6 phylogeography to test against
routes

### 4.1 L5.2.2.1.1.2.1.2 Gabon cohesive sub-clade (phase 26)

| Hypothesis | Route | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| H1 — Multi-hop coastal cabotage | Akan → Bénin → Niger Delta → Cameroon → Gabon | Possible, several centuries duration |
| **H2 — Pre-Bantu trans-equatorial connection** | Proto-Volta-Congo population spanning Akan + Gabon zones | **Preferred** (compatible with 76-SPDI age estimate ~3 000-5 000 BP) |
| H3 — Portuguese-mediated trans-shipment | São Tomé as intermediate | Possible but too recent (76 SPDIs too many) |

### 4.2 L5.2.2.1.1.2.1.1.1 Akan strict (phase 27)

| Hypothesis | Route | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Empire Ashanti / royaumes prédécesseurs centralisation | Internal Akan area expansion (no maritime route needed) | Consistent (8 SPDIs ~ 300-800 BP) |
| Atlantic export to Caribbean / USA via Elmina | Gold Coast → Jamaica + Caroline | Diaspora evidence absent (test was negative phase 30) |

**Interpretation** : Akan-strict sub-clade emerged **after**
the most active phase of Atlantic trade departures from Elmina
(1650-1800). Post-1750 Ashanti expansion would have created the
sub-clade in Kumasi after the major slave waves had already departed.

### 4.6 The Gullah/Geechee refuge

Phase 30 Mantel test was **globally negative** but identified one
clear refuge : **Sierra Leone + Senegambia → USA South**. Both regions
fed the Carolinas/Georgia rice plantations via Bance Island slave
factor (Sierra Leone) and Saint-Louis/Gorée (Senegal). The Sea Islands
of South Carolina and Georgia preserved a creole society (Gullah,
Geechee) that maintained more West African cultural traits than
mainland slave populations.

**MTBC consequence** : the Gullah/Geechee population may have
preserved the L5/L6 ancestral signature **better than mainland
Caribbean or Brazilian populations** where European/Portuguese
admixture and L4 replacement were stronger. This is consistent
with the 20 USA L5/L6 observations for L6.1.2.1.2 (Mel_Atlantic) +
the 52 Senegambia-derived strains.

## 5. Programmatic recommendations for L5/L6 phylogeography

1. **Test cabotage chains for L5.2.2.1.1.2.1.2 Gabon** : compare its
   intermediate sites (Bight of Benin coast, Bight of Biafra, Cameroon
   coast) for ancestral SPDIs. If found, H1 (multi-hop) is reinforced.
   If absent, H2 (pre-Bantu) is reinforced.

2. **Sample Cape Verde and São Tomé / Príncipe** programmatically.
   Both are critical transit hubs absent from current TB sequencing.

3. **Sample Gullah/Geechee Sea Islands** (South Carolina, Georgia)
   for L5/L6 sub-clades. If they preserve Sierra Leone/Senegambia
   ancestral diversity, this is a unique window into pre-Middle-Passage
   variation.

4. **Sample Aguda communities of Lagos/Ouidah/Porto-Novo**. These are
   Brazilian-returnee descendants and may show **L4 Brazilian + L5/L6
   African** admixture, signing the bi-directional Bahia-Lagos route.

5. **Sample trans-Saharan diaspora** in Maghreb (Algeria, Tunisia,
   Libya, southern Morocco). Some Maghreb L5/L6 cases could be
   descendants of trans-Saharan captives rather than recent migrants.
